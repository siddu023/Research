## 1. Executive summary

**Final all-in cost, ex-land: LOW $7.1M / EXPECTED $10.8M / HIGH $14.4M.**

The HIGH is a coherent stressed scenario (hard +20%, underground detention, 26-month schedule at 8.25%, high soft-cost percentages). The arithmetic sum of every line's HIGH is $16.4M and is shown in `work/TASKS.md` for transparency only.

**The prior $6-8.5M ex-land estimate does not survive.** It sits below the LOW of any structure a lender will finance. The four largest reasons, in order: the brief's owner-GC model is not financeable under SBA SOP 50 10 8 and NC G.S. 87-1, so a licensed construction manager and general conditions (~$0.55M) must be carried; ~$1.1M of scope was missing from the prior number (earthwork and on-site utilities, site electrical service, store FF&E and coolers, wash floor systems, off-site road improvements, signage); interim interest through 504 debenture funding is ~$0.46M, not the ~$0.2M a 12-month draw implies; and the wash ramp reserve a lender will require is ~$0.45M.

**Equity check (SBA 504, 13 CFR 120.910):**

| Case | TPC | Equity at 15% | Equity at 20% | Total cash incl. non-504 block, 15% / 20% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ex-land, EXPECTED | $9.94M | **$1.49M** | **$1.99M** | **$2.35M / $2.84M** |
| Ex-land, LOW | $6.53M | $0.98M | $1.31M | $1.53M / $1.85M |
| With $2.0M land, EXPECTED | $11.94M | **$1.79M** | **$2.39M** | **$2.65M / $3.24M** |

Plus ~$0.22M of owner liquidity to evidence at closing. The 15% path requires RS Fuels LLC to be the applicant with 24+ months of dealer-store revenue; a start-up applicant pays 20% because gas stations and car washes are special-purpose property. The 504 debenture cap ($5.0M) does not bind at EXPECTED; it binds only at the stressed HIGH with land.

**The revenue finding the owners must hear first.** At the RS Fuels per-store target of $60K/month combined fuel-plus-inside gross profit, this site cannot service its debt. Two independent models (planner and auditor) put debt-service coverage at 0.42-0.82x. Bankability at the lender's 1.25x requires roughly **$108K/month** combined GP ex-land (about $123K/month if land is financed in the stack) plus a 2,500-member wash. That is 1.8-2.0x the target and there is no trade-area evidence for it yet. This is the number one open risk (Section 7).

**Schedule:** 18 / **20** / 26 months from site control to opening, no rezoning. Duke Energy three-phase service (pad-mount transformers running 40-65 weeks) is the long pole and must be filed in month 1.

**Delivery model:** NC-licensed general contractor engaged as construction manager (agency, 5% fee) with owner-held fixed-price subcontracts and owner-furnished equipment packages (~$2.5M of tunnel, fuel, canopy, vacuums, FF&E, DCFC). Owner-as-GC is recorded as the rejected model, not an alternative.

**Recommended program:** 2.75 acres; 8 fueling positions under a 4,100 sf canopy, stubbed for 12; 4 double-wall FRP tanks, 57K gal; 4,000 sf store with a 600 sf kitchen; 120 ft belt-conveyor tunnel in a 150 x 40 ft pre-engineered shell with a 16-20 car stack; 10 central-vacuum stalls; Tesla Supercharger host application at $0 with a hard decision rule; 2 owned dual NACS/CCS DC fast stalls on a separate Duke make-ready transformer; 6 Wall Connectors.

### 3B. What a $5.5M QuikTrip listing is

Price equals net operating income divided by cap rate. A QT at $5.5M and 5.75-6.0% carries roughly $316-330K of rent. That rent was set in 2011-2012 as a sale-leaseback yield on QT's 2011 construction cost and bumped about 5% every five years. The listing is 2011 cost compounded through a cap rate. It is not 2026 replacement cost, it includes land, and it contains no tunnel, vacuums or EV.

The listings are evidence that buying an existing station is cheaper than building one. They are not evidence that this estimate should come down.

### 3D. The $4M 7-Eleven

A $4M 7-Eleven at a 5.1-5.8% cap carries $205-230K of rent: a 2,500-3,500 sf box on 1-1.5 acres, often 15-30 years old, 6-8 positions, little food program. Replacement cost of that box ex-land is roughly $2.5-3.5M. The $4M is land plus a small old building plus investment-grade credit. It says nothing about a 4,000 sf food-forward store with a tunnel.

### 3E. Standalone wash cross-check

The brand-new NC957 turnkey wash at $3.9M with land implies $2.7-3.1M ex-land. This plan's wash-side lines (A2-A5, P4, P5, share of site) sum to ~$3.0-3.4M. Consistent. The $14.5M three-year-old wash with 5,000 members shows stabilized washes trade at 5-8x EBITDA: value is created by membership, not bricks, which is also why lenders underwrite the ramp rather than the cost.

## 6. Financing plan

### 6A. SBA 504 rules verified (September 2026)

- Structure: bank 50% first lien / CDC debenture up to 40% / borrower equity at least 10%. Under 13 CFR 120.910 the equity is **15%** if the borrower has operated 2 years or less OR the property is limited/special-purpose (gas station, car wash), and **20%** if both. Applicant must be RS Fuels LLC with 24+ months of revenue to reach the 15% path.
- Debenture cap $5.0M ($5.5M for energy/manufacturing public-policy projects; LED canopy, reclaim and VFD dryers may qualify for the energy goal, CDC to confirm). 25-year debenture priced 6.54% on Sep 10, 2026. Fees about 2.65% of net debenture plus 0.5% bank participation plus 0.5-1% bank origination.
- Eligible: land, construction, A&E, appraisal and environmental, interim interest, closing costs, furniture and equipment, contingency up to 10% of construction (13 CFR 120.882). Not eligible: working capital, inventory, pre-opening payroll.
- Occupancy: 60% or more owner-occupied at closing and no more than 20% permanently leased to third parties (13 CFR 120.131). A Tesla pad at ~4% of the site fits, with an SNDA and CDC/bank consent.
- Equity may come from any non-SBA source; borrowed equity needs an outside repayment source; a seller note counts only if on full standby or subordinated (SOP 50 10 8).
- 504 and 7(a) were decoupled on Jul 4, 2026, so a 7(a) companion loan can stack for the non-504 block up to $10M combined.
- Borrower-as-contractor: permitted only if experienced and licensed, cost proven by two bids, and no profit earned; SBA describes DIY construction as "generally unsatisfactory." Lenders want a fixed-price or GMP contract with a qualified GC.
