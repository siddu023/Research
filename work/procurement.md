## 0. How to read this file

- Every line: LOW / EXPECTED / HIGH, then the source with date. "Quote-only" means no list price is published anywhere I could find; the range is triangulated from the cited cost guides, secondary-market listings, and forum bid reports, and must be replaced by 3 written quotes before the number is used for financing.
- Two subtotals are carried per system: **Cheapest-credible** (6 positions, 3 tanks, 100-ft chain-conveyor tunnel, individual vacuums, Tesla-funded EV only) and **Best-value** (8 positions, 4 tanks, 120-ft belt tunnel, central vacuums, 2 owned DCFC stalls). These are equipment + specialty install only; building shell, sitework, paving, utilities, soft costs belong to the architect/planner and are NOT in these subtotals.
- Web-search budget for this session was exhausted after ~35 queries; several vendor pages (Tesla, Duke, Gilbarco, MacNeil) returned 403. Where that blocked a number, it is marked **UNVERIFIED** and listed in section 7 for Phase 2 follow-up.

### 1.3 Brand image money vs unbranded — what is actually on offer (2025-26)

- What a brand/jobber contributes: NACS describes an "imaging allowance (loan)" plus signage/marketing support, and a wholesale price benchmarked to an index "plus a premium of a few cents" for brand support; typical contract 10 yrs (range 3-20) [NACS]. Image loans "amortize through fuel purchases" via a per-gallon surcharge over 10-15 yrs and are clawed back on early termination; "a remaining image loan balance reduces the proceeds available at sale" [GSG].
- Jobber marketing pages confirm the funding stack: "Capital investment and/or rebates available to upgrade dispensers, EPOS, lighting and signage" funded from "Major oil program funds, [jobber] funds, Operator funds, Leasing capital" [BF]. Marathon signs ~20 new-to-industry dealers/yr [CSP-MAR]. NC jobber verified: Sampson-Bladen Oil (BP/Exxon/Citgo, 50+ dealers) [SBO]. Quality Oil (Shell) and other NC jobbers are known but were not verified this session.
- Dollar amounts are NOT published by any major or jobber. Estimating basis (UNVERIFIED, must be replaced by 3 jobber term sheets): NTI image packages commonly cover canopy fascia/brand signage, dispenser graphics, price sign, and sometimes a contribution toward dispensers or POS, in the $75K-$250K range, amortized over 10 yrs against 1.5-2.5M gal/yr — equivalent to roughly 0.5-1.5 cpg, on top of a 1-3 cpg branded premium vs unbranded rack. Net effect on the build budget: LOW $0 (unbranded) / EXPECTED -$120K / HIGH -$250K of owner capex, traded for a 10-yr supply obligation and shortfall penalties of $0.02-0.05/gal (Compliance Hub, surfaced in search).
- Recommendation: run branded (Marathon/Exxon/BP via Sampson-Bladen or Quality Oil) and unbranded (jobber DTW) term sheets side by side; treat image money as a loan in the SBA sources-and-uses, not as equity. The 504 lender will read it that way regardless.

### 2.4 New vs certified-refurbished — where it is warranty-safe

- No manufacturer runs a formal "certified refurb" tunnel-package program that I could verify; the market is consignment (CarWashConsignment.com) with occasional 1-yr warranties on specific items.
- Warranty-safe to buy used/refurb: pay stations (ICS/Sonny's units common), vacuum turbines and arches (motors are commodity), RO/reclaim skids (membranes replaced anyway), dryer housings (re-motor), brush arches (re-cloth). Potential saving: 30-50% on those lines = $60K-$120K on the best-value case.
- Not warranty-safe: conveyor (belt/chain wear and controls), tunnel controller and relay electronics (support lifecycle), chemical delivery/manifolds. Buy new.
- Lender angle: SBA 504 collateral on used equipment is discounted; keep used items under ~15% of the equipment package.

## 4. Trades bid strategy (owner as GC)

### 4.1 What the GC fee actually is, and what you can keep

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial GC fee (overhead + profit) | 10-20% of project cost; 20-25% for complex, coordination-heavy projects; large projects lower (12% on $10M), small higher (18-22% on $500K TI) | [EB3-GC] Oct 7, 2025 |
| Construction manager (agency) fee | 5-15% | [EB3-GC] |
| 2026 average GC O&P | 21% (13% overhead + 8% profit) | [HG] |
| GC net pre-tax profit (what the market actually keeps) | 5-7% average; top firms 10% | CFMA benchmarks cited in [HG] |
| Owner-builder penalties | 5-10% higher sub/material pricing (no volume discounts); worked example: $1M project expected to save $200K, ended $158K OVER the GC bid after delays, owner time, insurance | [L4] May 30, 2024 |

Realistic owner-GC savings on a ~$5-6M hard-cost program: gross fee avoided 10-15% = $500-900K; give back 5-8% for general conditions you still pay (superintendent, temp power/fencing/toilets, builder's risk + GL, dumpsters, surveying, punch), and 3-5% for sub pricing premium and schedule slip. **Net realistic: +3% to +7% ($150-400K). Failure mode: -3% to -8% (-$150K to -$450K) plus 3-6 months of extra construction interest.** Best-value structure: hire an NC-licensed GC as CM-agency at 4-6% with a full-time superintendent, owners hold the subcontracts. Planner/auditor to verify (a) NC GS 87-1 owner-builder exemption applicability to a commercial build, and (b) that the 504 bank/CDC will close on an owner-GC structure at all — most require a licensed GC of record, bonding, and a fixed-price contract. This is a financing risk before it is a construction risk.
