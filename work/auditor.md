### 2b. What a $5.5M QT listing actually is

Price = NOI / cap. A QT at $5.5M and 5.75-6.0% cap carries ~$316-330K of rent. That rent was set in 2011-2012 as a sale-leaseback yield (typically 7-8%) on QT's **2011 cost**, then bumped 5% every 5 years. So: 2011 all-in cost ≈ $3.6-4.0M incl. land → 2011 rent ≈ $270-300K → 2026 rent ≈ $300-330K → capitalized at 5.75-6% = $5.0-5.7M. The listing price is 2011 construction cost compounded through a cap rate, not 2026 replacement cost. Construction costs since 2011 have risen far faster than 5%/5yr rent bumps (RLB: Charlotte +4.66% in the last year alone; the 2020-2023 inflation shock alone was ~30-40% cumulative in ENR/RLB indices).

### 2d. 7-Eleven at $4M

A $4M 7-Eleven at 5.1-5.8% cap carries $205-230K rent — a 2,500-3,500 SF box on 1-1.5 ac, often 15-30 years old, 6-8 positions, no food program to speak of, often a converted older site. Replacement cost of that box ex-land is roughly $2.5-3.5M (Terrapin "standard 3,000 SF, 4 dispensers $2.2-2.8M" + NC metro adder). The $4M is land + a small old box + investment-grade credit (7-Eleven AA-). It says nothing about a 4,250 SF food-forward store with a tunnel.

### 2e. Standalone express tunnel cross-check

- NC957 (new, turnkey at C.O., **with land**) at $3.9M → ex-land ~$2.7-3.1M. My e+f+g+share ≈ $3.0-3.4M. Consistent; if anything the listing suggests my wash-side HIGH is conservative and LOW is achievable with a PEMB shell and a mid-spec package.

## 5. Owner-GC savings — evidence and the priced failure mode

### What a GC actually charges

| Evidence | Figure | Source (date) |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial GC fee | 10-20% of project; complex 20-25% | EB3 Construction (2025-26) |
| Combined overhead & profit, 2026 average | ~21% (13% overhead + 8% profit) — residential-skewed | HomeGuide (2026) |
| Well-managed GC pre-tax net | 5-7%, top firms 10% | Next Insurance (2026) |
| Honest commercial split | fee/profit 4-8% + general conditions 6-10% (super, temp facilities, insurance, cleanup, safety, scheduling) | industry norm; EB3 |

The only part an owner-GC removes is the **fee (4-8% of ~$7.2M hard = $290-575K)**. General conditions do not disappear: the owner must still hire a superintendent/CM ($120-180K over 14-18 months), rent temp power/fencing/toilets/dumpsters, buy builder's risk, and pay for scheduling and lien administration. **Net realistic saving: 2-4% of hard = $145-290K**, and only if the owners have built before.

### Lender / SBA position (dated)

- SBA SOP 50 10 8 (eff. Jun 1, 2025): borrower-as-contractor is permitted only if the CDC documents that (a) the borrower is "experienced in the type of construction and has all appropriate licenses," (b) cost is "the same as or less than what an unaffiliated contractor would charge as evidenced by two bids," and (c) the borrower "will not earn a profit." SBA states DIY construction "ha[s] proven to be generally unsatisfactory and can cause problems with lien waivers and mechanics liens." (Partner ESI summary; FFCFC 504 Q&A: DIY Construction, 2025-26.)
- 504 construction lenders want "complete architectural and engineering plans and a final fixed-price or guaranteed maximum price contract with a qualified general contractor" (Alloy Development, 2026).
- NC licensing: general contractor license required for projects >$40,000 (NC G.S. 87-1); owner exemption applicability to a commercial build for the owner's own operating business needs counsel — **OPEN**.

### Priced failure mode

| Failure | Cost per month of slip | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Construction interest on ~$6-7M interim balance | $40-45K | 7.75-8% (§3) |
| Carry: property tax, insurance, utilities, security | $10-15K | §4 opex |
| Lost NOI (Flagship case stabilized $95K/mo; yr-1 ~$50-70K/mo) | $50-95K | §4 |
| Wash membership ramp deferred (each month of delay pushes the 1,000-member mark) | $15-25K of future EBITDA | MMCG ramp |
| **Total** | **$115-180K per month** | |

A 2-month slip erases the entire realistic owner-GC saving; a 3-month slip costs more than a full GC fee. Sub-coordination failures on a fuel/wash site are specific: UST contractor (40 CFR 280.20 certified) vs civil vs canopy vs electrical vs tunnel-equipment installer have hard sequencing dependencies (tank pit before paving; equipment room MEP before conveyor; Duke transformer lead times of 6-12+ months). Two bids proving owner cost ≤ GC cost also means the owners must run a full bid anyway.

**Auditor position:** budget a licensed GC or CM-at-risk at a negotiated fee (4-6%, subs bid open-book) and capture savings instead through **owner-furnished equipment packages** (tunnel, fuel system, canopy, vacuums ≈ $2.3M) which avoids GC markup of 5-10% on those packages ($115-230K) — a comparable saving with a fixed-price contract lenders accept. I carry 12% contingency for owner-GC vs 8-10% with a GC; the 2-4% contingency difference alone consumes the claimed saving. Any agent claiming 10-20% owner-GC savings must show two bids, a licensed super, and a lender term sheet accepting the structure.
