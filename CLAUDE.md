# RS Fuels — Flagship Station Development Plan

## What this project is

RS Fuels LLC (50/50 partnership, North Carolina) is planning a ground-up flagship site combining, on one lot:

1. **Gas station** — 6-8 fueling positions under canopy, 3-4 double-wall USTs
2. **Convenience store** — ~4,000-4,500 sq ft with food program
3. **Express tunnel car wash** — 100-120 ft conveyor tunnel, membership model
4. **7-10 free vacuum stations** under canopy
5. **EV charging** — Tesla Supercharger host application (Tesla-funded, for Tesla map visibility), 2 dual NACS/CCS DC fast stalls (after Duke Energy make-ready), 4-6 Tesla Wall Connector destination chargers

**Positioning:** one-stop destination — customer psychology is that a 20-30 min charge or fuel stop pairs naturally with a full wash, driving inside sales and wash memberships. The wash and inside sales carry the margin; fuel and charging are the traffic magnets.

## Delivery model

- Owners act as their own GC / construction manager
- **Everything subcontracted** — civil, fuel system, building shell, tunnel equipment install, electrical, MEP
- Location: Raleigh or Charlotte metro growth corridor, North Carolina
- Land is EXCLUDED from the construction budget (priced separately: assume 2.5-3 acres)

## The core question this team must answer

Produce a meticulous, line-item, market-verified budget and execution plan. The owners have seen:
- Existing QT-format stations listed at $5-6M including land
- 7-Eleven stations listed as low as $4M
- A prior all-in estimate of $6-8.5M ex-land for this full program

The team must reconcile listing prices (cap-rate-priced existing assets, no tunnel) vs replacement cost (ground-up with tunnel), find every legitimate cost reduction, and produce a defensible final number with options at each line.

## Financial framing

- Financing target: SBA 504 (50% bank / 40% CDC / 10-20% borrower equity). Gas stations and car washes are special-purpose property; owners are early-stage — assume 15-20% equity requirement. Size the equity check.
- Per-store stabilized target across RS Fuels: ~$60K/month combined gross profit (fuel + inside). The flagship adds wash membership revenue on top — model it.
- Sequencing context: RS Fuels' near-term entry is low-capital dealer/commission-agent stores (SEI Fuels, GPM dealer program, local jobbers); the flagship is the later owned build funded partly by those stores' cash flow. The plan should state what operating history and financials lenders will want to see first.

## Ground rules for every agent

- Every dollar figure gets a LOW / EXPECTED / HIGH range and a named source or comp (published 2025-2026 cost data, vendor list pricing, utility program docs, SBA rules). No single-point guesses.
- Web-search to verify current numbers before asserting them. Costs move; do not rely on memory.
- North Carolina specifics matter: NCDEQ UST permitting, Duke Energy EV make-ready program, NC stormwater detention rules, Charlotte (Mecklenburg) vs Raleigh (Wake) permitting differences.
- When two agents disagree on a number, they must argue it out in the shared task list and record the resolution and reasoning — do not silently average.
- Flag every commonly-omitted cost: utility service upgrades, stormwater detention, Phase I/II environmental, construction interest, signage, POS, initial inventory, pre-opening payroll, 3-6 months working capital, contingency.

## Deliverable

A single `FLAGSHIP-PLAN.md` at repo root containing:
1. Executive summary with the final all-in ex-land range and equity check needed
2. Line-item budget table (component → options → low/expected/high → source/comp → recommendation)
3. Comparison table vs market comps (QT listing, 7-Eleven listing, standalone express tunnel sales, published new-build data) explaining every gap
4. Value-engineering menu: each cut, what it saves, what it costs the business
5. Phased execution schedule (months 0-18) with permitting critical path
6. Financing plan: SBA 504 structure, equity requirement, what lenders need to see, fallback structures
7. Open risks and unresolved disagreements between agents, stated honestly
